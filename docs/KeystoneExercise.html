Lets now look at the difference in environmental conditions across these zones.

Salinity

There is a clear salinity gradient across the zones, with the upper estuary being fresher and the lower estuary being more saline. This is consistent with what we would expect in an estuarine system, where freshwater inputs from rivers mix with seawater from the ocean. This salinity gradient could be an important driver of fish abundance patterns across these zones, as different species may have different salinity preferences or tolerances.

Turbidity

There is minimal tubidity differences across the zones, with all zones having similar median turbidity values. This suggests that turbidity may not be a major driver of fish abundance patterns across these zones, although it could still have some influence on certain species that are more sensitive to turbidity levels.

Temperature

There is a slight temperature gradient across the zones, with the upper estuary being slightly warmer than the lower estuary. However, the differences in median temperature across the zones are relatively small. This suggests that temperature may not be a strong driver of fish abundance patterns across these zones.

Salinity is the most pronounced environmental gradient across these zones, and is likely to be the most important driver of fish abundance patterns in this estuarine system.
